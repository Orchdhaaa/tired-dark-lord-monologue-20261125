One 100-word monologue for a fantasy villain who is secretly just very tired: "Come in. Leave the sword by the door; I have counted it thrice and it is a sword. Sit. The tea is cold, the map older than your grandfather, and the invasion, the invasion. Thirty years of banners and oaths, and then the scouts report a toll on the north road. A toll. So we wait. Not mercy. I have read the lists twice and the grain runs out Tuesday. Drink. When you leave, tell the bards I laughed, low, and the torches guttered. Shut the door quietly. The hinges squeak, and I have heard enough squeaking for one lifetime."

The tiredness lives in the pauses, the repeated counting, the deflected invasion and the squeaking hinges, never in a plain claim of weariness. He is still a villain: thirty years of banners and oaths, a scheme on hold only because the grain runs out Tuesday, and a last line that stings.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

`main.ts` writes the monologue to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (or `/output/entry.txt` by default), verifying first that it is one line of about 100 words and 1–600 characters with no dash, that it never announces tiredness, and that it names the scheme and its history.
